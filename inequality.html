Inequality: the Lorenz curve and Gini coefficient

Line everyone up from poorest to richest and add up their incomes as you go. If income were shared equally, that running total would be a straight line. The further it sags, the more unequal the society.

The idea

The Lorenz curve plots the cumulative share of income (vertical) earned by the poorest x% of people (horizontal). With perfect equality, the poorest 20% earn 20%, the poorest 40% earn 40%, and so on — the diagonal. Real curves sag below it.

The Gini coefficient turns the curve into one number: the area between the equality line and the Lorenz curve, divided by the whole triangle under the equality line. It runs from 0 (everyone equal) to 1 (one person has everything).

Two things to keep in mind. First, it matters which income you measure: market income before taxes is much more unequal than disposable income after taxes and benefits, so the gap shows how much a country redistributes. Second, very different distributions can have the same Gini — that's why statisticians also report ratios such as S80/S20 (income of the richest fifth divided by the poorest fifth).

Eurostat publishes both measures for every EU country each year from the EU-SILC survey, so you can compare Latvia with its neighbours.

Try this

  1. Click Perfect equality, then raise just the richest group. Watch the curve sag and the Gini climb.
  2. Try raising the middle groups instead of the top. The Gini changes less — it's most sensitive to the middle of the distribution.
  3. Look for two different settings with the same Gini but a different S80/S20 ratio.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the Gini as the share of income held by the rich. It's an area ratio, not a share.
  • Comparing a country's market-income Gini with another's disposable-income Gini.

Key formulas

Gini = A / (A + B)

A: gap between equality line and Lorenz curve; B: area under Lorenz curve

With 5 groups: Gini = 1 − Σ 0.2 × (Lₖ + Lₖ₋₁)

L = cumulative income share (as a fraction)

S80/S20 = top 20% income ÷ bottom 20%
