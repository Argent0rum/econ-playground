Production possibilities and opportunity cost

An economy has limited workers, machines and land. The production possibilities frontier shows the best combinations it can make — and every point on it involves giving something up.

Scenarios

The idea

The production possibilities frontier (PPF) shows every combination of two goods an economy can produce if it uses all its resources efficiently. Points inside are possible but wasteful; points outside are impossible with today's resources and technology.

Moving along the frontier means choosing. The opportunity cost of more food is the tech you give up to get it — the slope of the curve at that point. Because the curve bows outward, that cost rises as you specialise: the first workers moved into farming are natural farmers, the last ones are engineers who are terrible at it.

Economic growth pushes the whole frontier outward: more workers, more capital, better skills or new technology. Growth that improves only one industry stretches the frontier mostly in one direction.

Points inside the frontier represent unemployment or inefficiency. Getting back to the frontier, for example by recovering from a recession, gives you more of everything without any trade-off.

Common mistakes

  • Treating opportunity cost as money. It's the next-best thing you give up, measured in the other good.
  • Drawing a straight-line PPF and then talking about increasing costs. A straight line means constant opportunity cost.
