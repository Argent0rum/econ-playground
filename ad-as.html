Aggregate demand and aggregate supply

The AD–AS model is the macro version of supply and demand: total spending in the economy against total production, with the price level instead of a single price.

Scenarios

The idea

Aggregate demand (AD) is total spending on a country's output — consumption, investment, government purchases and net exports — at each price level. It slopes down mainly because a higher price level reduces real wealth and makes exports less competitive.

Short-run aggregate supply (SRAS) slopes up because some costs, especially wages, are fixed in contracts for a while: when prices rise, firms' margins widen and they produce more. Long-run aggregate supply is vertical at potential output — what the economy produces when its labour and capital are used at normal rates.

A demand shock moves AD: output and prices move in the same direction. A supply shock moves SRAS: an energy price spike raises prices and cuts output (stagflation), as Europe saw in 2022.

The gap between actual and potential output is the output gap. A positive gap creates inflation pressure; a negative gap means unemployment. In the long run wages adjust and the economy drifts back to potential, but “the long run” can take years — which is the case for active fiscal and monetary policy.

Common mistakes

  • Treating AD like a single market's demand curve. It slopes down for different reasons (wealth, interest rates, exchange rates), not substitution between goods.
  • Forgetting that a supply shock moves output and prices in opposite directions.
